It started with a simple problem: the tools I used to run a household couldn't keep up.

A planner for the schedule. A spreadsheet for grades. A chore whiteboard nobody updates. That's not a system, it's duct tape.

I ran on HomeHub for a while. It covered the basics, but it had nothing for homeschool records, or for the health side of a household. I needed grades, assignments, and records in the same place as the household calendar, not in a separate app. I also needed a shared calendar that stayed reliable. And the options that came close cost more than a family should have to pay just to stay organized.

Most family organizers stop at reminders. Teaching at home is an ongoing record, so the record has to live with the rest of the week.

That's when the job became clear. I didn't need one more app for one more slice of the day. I needed one place where what you teach, what you track, and what you do today all live together. So I built it, and called it Domi Ops, short for Domicile Operations.

## What it does today

[Domi Ops](https://domi-ops.com/) is a household hub for homeschool families. Classes, assignments, and a real gradebook sit next to the family calendar, chores, and the rest of the house. Each person gets their own login, under one household plan.

[School](https://domi-ops.com/homeschool) is a real record, not a reminder list. Students see their own work, and you see and grade everything. The gradebook uses weighted categories and tracks the whole year instead of resetting every Monday. Choice and short-answer questions can score automatically; written answers stay yours to review. You tap the days school happened, log hours as you go, and print a transcript on your own grading scale. It's a place to plan, assign, and record, not a curriculum. The transcript is prepared by you, not an official district document.

The household gets one shared week view. School overlays layer on top, and you can import a Google calendar so outside events don't become a second source of truth.

The rest of the house is included, and you can turn modules on as you need them. Chores have streaks and quests. Shopping lists group by aisle, recurring items refill themselves, and you can snap a receipt when you're done. You can set a monthly budget per category and get a nudge before you blow past it. Notes are markdown, and they can go straight into Household Drive when they need attachments. Drive is one folder tree for the files school, notes, and notices touch.

There's a [health side](https://domi-ops.com/health) too: medications, appointments, and vitals, shared with exactly who you choose. One reminder can cover a group of doses instead of a ping for each one. You can pause a medication and keep its dose history. Compatible Withings devices can import readings, workouts, and activity and sleep summaries. On Domi Ops Cloud, you can also link a household member to a MyAllyFile emergency profile so current medications copy over when they change. That link only goes one way.

## How you can run it

It's the same app either way. You can [self-host](https://domi-ops.com/pricing) for free under an MIT license, with every module included. You run it on your own server, and you manage setup, updates, and backups. File storage depends on your server.

Or you can use Domi Ops Cloud: $12 a month or $120 a year for one household, with a 14-day trial that does require a card. That plan includes all modules, 25 GB of household file storage, and managed hosting and software updates. Adults and children use their own logins, with roles and sharing controls. If you want to look around first, the [shared demo](https://demo.domi-ops.com) is a live sandbox with sample data and no card required.
