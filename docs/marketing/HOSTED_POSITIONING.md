# Hosted household positioning

Reviewed 2026-10-17. Lead with homeschool households. Keep health routines as a
reason the same household may value Domi Ops more, with a separate Health page for
caregiver discovery. Sell one Cloud subscription at $12/month or $120/year. Keep
self-hosting available without making server administration the first purchase task.

The public headline is: **The household hub for homeschool families.** The supporting
promise is concrete: teaching work, family schedules, everyday household work and
health routines in one place, with separate logins and explicit sharing controls.
This is a positioning hypothesis, not validated demand or proven differentiation.

## Current alternatives

Official vendor pages checked on the review date:

| Alternative | Current published offer | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Homeschool Planet](https://homeschoolplanet.com/homeschool-planet-subscriptions/) | Single-family $9.95/month or $84.95/year; 30-day trial without a card | We charge more annually and ask for a card sooner. School utility and setup must be immediately clear. |
| [Syllabird](https://www.syllabird.com/pricing) | Essential $6/month or $60/year, 50 GB; Premium $9/month or $90/year, 250 GB; shareable courses, rescheduling and a 14-day no-card trial | Storage and basic School feature counts do not justify our premium. Course reuse and rescheduling are expected workflows. |
| [FamilyWall](https://www.familywall.com/premium.html?lang=en) | Family calendar sync, meal planning, budgets, schedules and shared organization | Generic family organization already has established alternatives. Do not claim an empty category. |

Do not claim Domi Ops is cheapest, uniquely private, medically compliant, or the only
family app with files and budgets. The code supports role-based School work,
structured health routines, private sharing controls and an operator-assisted
self-host exit. None of that proves competitors cannot serve the same buyer.

## Offer and page decisions

- Cloud is the primary navigation and page CTA. Demo is the low-commitment second
  step. When checkout is disabled, say access is limited and give a working support
  contact. Do not present a nonfunctional trial button.
- State the card-required, auto-renewing 14-day trial beside the price and CTA.
  Scheduled cancellation keeps paid access until the period ends; expired access
  preserves permission-limited reading and reports. Returning customers do not get
  another free trial. Do not imply cancellation deletes data.
- Include School, Calendar, Health, Drive, Goals and core household planning in the
  single plan, with 25 GB of household file storage. Show household role logins,
  rather than a per-person price. Do not advertise the unbuilt dedicated Family tier.
- Describe observable outcomes and permission limits. Keep database/provider terms
  out of purchase copy; put operator requirements in technical documentation.
- Google login and optional integrations are separate flows. Google verification
  remains a release gate. Families can use local School and Health workflows without
  connecting Google. Do not advertise an approved integration before acceptance.
- Course reuse now copies categories, assignments and native tests into undated
  drafts, without student history. Bulk date changes require a preview and exclude
  submitted work. A curriculum marketplace, public course exchange, lesson-plan
  library and automatic vacation-aware pacing remain unbuilt; do not imply parity.

## Evidence needed before expanding spend or tiers

Run a small, consented pilot with homeschool households, including some that manage
health routines and some that do not. These are proposed research questions, not
results: what do they use today, what did they replace, can they create one real
workflow without help, does another household member participate, and why do they
keep or cancel after a paid renewal? Record assisted setup time and recurring support
effort. Ask what specifically earns $120/year over the alternatives above.

Use [the measurement plan](../HOSTED_MEASUREMENT.md); do not infer willingness to pay
from feature completion or a synthetic test. Reconsider the School lead if real
caregiver cohorts activate and retain better. Reconsider card friction only after
measuring qualified-demo-to-paid conversion and support/abuse cost. Do not add a
second paid tier until customer demand and service cost support it.
